A mountain is an elevated portion of the surface of a planet, generally with steep sides that show
significant exposed bedrock. Although definitions vary, a mountain is usually considered higher than a hill
typically rising 610 metres (2,000 ft)[1] or more above the surrounding land.
It may either have a limited summit area or be a smaller plateau with high elevation
and steep sides. A few mountains are isolated summits, but most occur
in mountain ranges.[2][page needed] Mountains are formed through tectonic forces, erosion, or volcanism,[2][page needed]
which act on time scales of up to tens of millions of years.[3]
Once mountain building ceases, mountains are slowly leveled through the action of weathering, through slumping
and other forms of mass wasting, as well as through erosion by rivers and glaciers.[4]
